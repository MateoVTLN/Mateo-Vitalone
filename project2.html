Folk-Bucker

An electric guitar pickup… inside a folk acoustic

Electronics Electroacoustics Woodworking

Two years ago I bought a Cort folk acoustic guitar. On its own it's a fine instrument: comfortable to play for electric-guitar aficionados, a mahogany body with just enough finish, functional tuners…

Around 2022 I became fascinated by Nirvana's MTV Unplugged live album. The sound of Kurt Cobain's Martin guitar struck me immediately — clean as well as through effects. So I asked myself: could part of the electronics of an electric guitar be built into a folk guitar to get a similar result?

At my disposal: a bridge-position humbucker (15 kΩ impedance), a 500 kΩ logarithmic Alpha potentiometer, a 2-position on/off switch, a piece of pickguard from an SG, and a few optional cosmetic parts (volume knob, graduated washer and a PVC plate acting as a front control plate).

Preamble

The inspiration

Shortly after discovering Nirvana's "MTV Unplugged" live album, I looked into the instruments used during that performance. The sound coming from Kurt Cobain's guitar pleased me as much as it surprised me: he was playing an acoustic Martin fitted with electric-guitar pickups!

Having just acquired my first folk guitar at the time, I set myself the challenge of reproducing some of that guitar's electronic features — installing a pickup and a volume control.

Step 1

Preparations

First, the old strings had to go, leaving the top of the instrument and the sound hole fully exposed. Note that this modification is only possible on instruments with steel strings — not nylon — since the pickup relies on the magnetic field disturbed by vibrating steel strings.

Then came the layout of the necessary drillings: the 500 kΩ log potentiometer, the 6.35 mm jack output, the DPDT switch, and the pickup height-adjustment screws.

The potentiometer sits close to the playing hand, near the bridge — Fender Telecaster style.

Step 2

Adaptation and assembly

The humbucker was mounted on a piece of SG pickguard trimmed to span the sound hole, which lets the pickup sit under the strings at the right height without touching the soundboard. The height-adjustment screws go through the plate, so the distance between the pole pieces and the strings stays adjustable.

The controls were then assembled: the volume potentiometer near the bridge, and on the lower bout a small home-made control plate carrying the on/off switch and the 6.35 mm jack output. Inside the body, the pickup signal runs through the switch to the volume pot and out to the jack; a ground wire connects to the bridge to ground the strings and keep hum at bay.

Once strung up again, the guitar was tested through a VOX ToneLab and a Marshall stack — clean and with effects. The result speaks for itself in the videos below.
